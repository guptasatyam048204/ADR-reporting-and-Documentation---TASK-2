## 11. Causality Assessment

### Assessment

**Probable**

### Basis for assessment

The suspected relationship between chlorpromazine and the adverse reaction
is supported by:

1. A reasonable temporal relationship between starting chlorpromazine and
   development of the reaction.
2. Development of clinical jaundice and abnormal liver-function parameters.
3. Withdrawal of the suspected medication.
4. Improvement in clinical symptoms following withdrawal.
5. Improvement in liver-function parameters after withdrawal.
6. No reported rechallenge.

Diclofenac is a potential concomitant/confounding medication and should
therefore be documented in the ADR report.

---

## 12. Final ADR Summary

A 65-year-old male patient weighing 72 kg developed upper abdominal pain,
nausea and jaundice after approximately four weeks of treatment with
chlorpromazine (Largactil) 400 mg orally once daily at bedtime for psychotic
attacks.

Laboratory investigations demonstrated elevated alkaline phosphatase,
ALT and total bilirubin. Chlorpromazine and concomitant diclofenac were
withdrawn. The patient's symptoms and laboratory abnormalities subsequently
improved.

The suspected adverse drug reaction was **chlorpromazine-associated
hepatotoxicity/liver injury presenting with jaundice, abdominal pain,
nausea and elevated liver-function parameters**.

The causality assessment was classified as **Probable**.

## 14. Conclusion

This case demonstrates the documentation of a suspected adverse drug reaction
using a structured pharmacovigilance reporting approach.

The principal suspected medication was chlorpromazine, with diclofenac
documented as a concomitant medication. The clinical presentation,
laboratory findings, withdrawal of medication and subsequent improvement
support a probable association between chlorpromazine exposure and the
reported liver injury.
